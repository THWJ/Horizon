### [Terence Tao: Math 2.0 Should De-emphasize Solving Famous Problems](https://www.solidot.org/story?sid=85545) ⭐️ 8.0/10

OpenAI released a report claiming that an unreleased frontier model solved hundreds of mathematics problems, including the four-dimensional Kakeya conjecture; the supplied account does not independently verify that claim. In response, UCLA mathematician Terence Tao argued that mathematics should enter a &quot;2.0&quot; era in which solving famous open problems carries less central weight. He said AI-generated proofs typically come from prompters with little mathematical interest who only care about the act of solving and cannot attend seminars or discuss results with domain experts, and that AI companies are pushing pioneering research toward secrecy to avoid being scooped. Tao proposed that the field instead measure progress more broadly by valuing academic exposition, community building, and the opening of new research directions.

rss · 奇客Solidot · Oct 8, 07:35

**「Background」** &quot;Math 2.0&quot; is Tao&\#x27;s own label for a reorientation of mathematics&\#x27; incentives rather than a new technology; in related commentary he characterized OpenAI&\#x27;s simultaneous release of roughly 300 proofs as an unsustainable, large-scale harvesting of open problems that leaves whole areas of mathematics less fertile than when those problems are worked through by the community over time. OpenAI describes the system behind the report as an unreleased frontier model, so the claimed solutions have not been independently verified.

**「Impact」** For working mathematicians, the concrete near-term effect Tao describes is a shift in publication behavior: the risk of being scooped by AI labs is pushing researchers to keep pioneering work private, while AI-driven results arrive in bulk — OpenAI released 722 AI-generated mathematics papers on 7 October \(tool-3-1\), after an earlier model reportedly resolved one of the field&\#x27;s biggest open problems \(tool-3-3\). He argues the field should accordingly weigh exposition, community building, and the opening of new research directions above being first to solve a famous problem.

### [ASML and ZEISS: Hyper NA EUV targets 5nm half-pitch in about 10 years](https://www.ithome.com/1/010/829.htm) ⭐️ 7.0/10

ASML and its optics partner ZEISS said in an article published October 1 that Hyper NA EUV systems with a numerical aperture of at least 0.75 are expected to support 5nm half-pitch optical resolution and to arrive in roughly 10 years. The main technical challenge is a lower usable depth of field, which would require thinner photoresists along with improved focus control on the scanner, wafer flatness, and process control. A 0.75 NA optical system would be only slightly larger than existing High NA EUV tools, preserving cleanroom and equipment compatibility, whereas extending to 0.85 NA would greatly increase system size and make it less attractive. With optical and mechanical improvements, the existing mask infrastructure is expected to be reusable on Hyper NA EUV without major changes, and the light source is also expected to be reused.

rss · IT之家 · Oct 9, 02:29

**「Background」** The current leading EUV generation, High NA EUV, operates at a numerical aperture of 0.55, and Hyper NA is framed as the next extension beyond it by raising NA to at least 0.75. ASML and Carl Zeiss SMT laid out that path in a technical paper published October 1, 2026, which projects a 5 nm half-pitch tool roughly a decade out based on extrapolation of past resolution trends.

**「Impact」** For chipmakers planning beyond current High NA EUV, the projected mask and light-source reuse at 0.75 NA limits how much of the surrounding infrastructure would have to be replaced. The counterpart is process development: thinner resists and tighter focus, wafer-flatness, and process control would need to be qualified before the lower usable depth of field can be managed, and the source&\#x27;s own analysis treats 0.85 NA as unattractive because of system growth.

### [Google says attackers hijacked .gh, .sl, and .as country-code domains](https://www.solidot.org/story?sid=85549) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Google&\#x27;s security blog reported that attackers hijacked several of its country-code top-level domains — .gh \(Ghana\), .sl \(Sierra Leone\), and .as \(American Samoa\) — by modifying authoritative DNS records, and also obtained unauthorized HTTPS certificates for the affected domains. Google stated the attack did not compromise its own systems, and that Chrome quickly blocked the certificates. Google added that, given the nature of such attacks, it does not consider the issuing CA to have violated the rules, and it advised organizations using .gh, .sl, or .as domains to inspect Certificate Transparency log entries for unexpected certificates.

rss · 奇客Solidot · Oct 8, 10:31

**「Background」** Country-code top-level domains such as .gh, .sl and .as are delegated namespaces: their registries run the authoritative DNS servers that answer queries for every domain beneath them, and certificate authorities commonly validate a certificate request by confirming control through those same DNS records. An attacker who gains control of a ccTLD&\#x27;s registry or registrar infrastructure can therefore obtain certificates for domains it does not own, which is the mechanism at work here. Reporting on the disclosure describes the compromise as spanning three ccTLD registries and as yielding unauthorized certificates not only for Google but also for domains belonging to other organizations.

**「What affected domain owners should do」** Because the attackers modified authoritative DNS records for .gh, .sl, and .as before obtaining unauthorized but technically valid certificates for Google domains and domains belonging to other major brands, organizations using those ccTLDs should audit Certificate Transparency logs for unexpected certificates, as Google advised. Chrome&\#x27;s block of the affected certificates is a client-side mitigation, so any relying party outside that blocklist should assume the certificates remained usable until revoked.
